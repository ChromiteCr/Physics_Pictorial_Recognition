
######## research:isef-rules
## SUMMARY
Regeneron ISEF 2027 runs May 8-14, 2027 at the Los Angeles Convention Center. Students can only get there through a Society-affiliated fair. Those fairs must finish by April 12, 2027, and the Society's own calendar has no student deadline on Nov 15, so the student's 2026-11-15 date has to come from the affiliated fair. Chinese fair pages show paperwork deadlines in Nov-Dec. The Sichuan fair is only for international-school students, and a 2022 page sent Chinese-school students to CASTIC (fair code CHN 001); I could not re-verify that this still holds. The 2026-27 rules allow at most 12 continuous months of research, none before Jan 2026, and the start date is the day data collection begins. Every project needs Forms 1, 1A, a research plan written by the student, and Form 1B (student and parent), all signed before experimentation, plus the new Form 2A. The plan has to cover every phase, because nothing can change after the affiliated fair. If anyone other than the student tests or uses the app, or if the project collects surveys or opinions (teacher ratings, for example) or classmates' data, it counts as human-participant research. That needs a 3-member school IRB to approve it before recruitment starts, and minors need written parental permission and their own assent. Testing done only by the student, with no hazard, is exempt. The Companion Guide also says an app's results should not be shown to participants and strongly advises against AI chatbots or unscripted AI replies to them. That clashes directly with showing AI feedback to students in class. Continuations must be declared on Form 7 and must be a substantial expansion. Generative AI must be cited. It may not write the plan, abstract or poster, or produce citations. AI-written code has to be identified, with a log of the prompts. Form 2A asks for an AI prompt log. Science and engineering projects are judged on the same weights: 10/15/20/20/35, with the 35 split into poster 10 and interview 25. The best category is ROBO (Machine Learning, or Cognitive Systems). SFTD is the fallback; there is no SOFT category any more.

- [medium] (Best-fit category (argument)) Best fit is ROBO, subcategory Machine Learning, or Cognitive Systems if the pitch is a system that extends a teacher's diagnostic expertise. Reasons:
- The core deliverable is machine intelligence that replaces teacher intervention: it recognizes an arbitrary experiment from a photo and attributes error causes using physics models plus a language model.
- The teacher requires a genuine model-level upgrade to the vision model or the LLM, and ML judges are the ones placed to credit that.

Alternatives:
- SFTD-Algorithms is the fallback if the contribution is mainly pipeline or software design.
- SFTD-Online Learning targets learning outside traditional settings, which fits poorly for in-lab use.
- PHYS-Mechanics only fits if the headline result is a new physics finding about error mechanisms; physicist judges may undervalue the ML side.
- EBED would need sensors or signals.
- BEHA would need a learning-outcomes study as the main question.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/categories-and-subcategories/all-categories/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/categories-and-subcategories/>
- [high] (Human participants: definition) Following 45 CFR 46, a human participant is a living person about whom the researcher obtains data or samples through intervention or interaction, or obtains identifiable private information. The listed examples include:
- interacting with people for the study;
- surveys, questionnaires or tests of any kind;
- studies where the researcher is the subject;
- testing of a student-designed invention, prototype or computer application by anyone other than the student researcher;
- data that are not de-identified;
- behavioral observations that involve interaction, take place in non-public or restricted-access settings, or record personally identifiable information.

The Rules FAQ adds that any project where someone other than the student researcher(s) tests or uses the student's invention, product or app is a human-participant study needing IRB pre-approval.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/>
- [high] (IRB: when it is required and its timing) Every human-participant study needs IRB approval before any interaction with participants, recruitment included. A school IRB needs at least 3 members:
- an educator who is not the Adult Sponsor;
- a school administrator;
- a medical or mental-health professional (e.g., doctor, nurse, psychologist, licensed social worker).

The Adult Sponsor, parents or relatives, the Qualified Scientist and the Direct Supervisor may not serve. The IRB records its risk level and consent decisions on Form 4 with all 3 signatures dated before experimentation; missing checkmarks or signatures can make the project fail to qualify. Any change to the plan needs re-approval before work with participants resumes. The fair SRC can override an IRB it judges wrong. When human studies are IRB-approved, no separate prior SRC review is needed.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/roles-and-responsibilities-of-students-and-adults/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Forms/All.pdf>
- [high] (Exemption: testing a student-designed computer application) Testing a student-designed invention, prototype, computer application, engineering/design project or consumer product is exempt from IRB pre-approval and human-participant forms only when "the student researcher (or researchers if a team) is the only person testing" (Society for Science, Human Participants rules) and the testing poses no health or safety hazard. A single adult (guardian, Adult Sponsor, QS or DS) may be the tester only when the testing requires an adult, such as age-restricted activities like driving, and then replaces the student instead of adding to them. IRB pre-approval is required if anyone beyond that one tester is involved, or if any human variable is introduced (e.g., the tester's sleep, strength or endurance).  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/>
- [high] (Other IRB exemptions) Also exempt:
- reviews of publicly available or published pre-existing data sets that involve no interaction with people;
- observations in unrestricted public places with no interaction, no manipulation of the environment and no identifiable data recorded;
- pre-existing data received already de-identified, provided the professional supplying it certifies the de-identification and privacy compliance in writing and the affiliated-fair SRC checks that documentation.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/>
- [medium] (Surveys, opinions and teacher ratings) The IRB requirement explicitly covers surveys about the potential use of, or opinions on, a student's invention or product. The only carve-out is professional feedback from experts in the field received before experimentation. The Engineering & Invention guide lists asking anyone for opinions or suggestions on the design, at any point, as a trigger to check the human-participant rules. Inference: teachers rating system outputs as evaluation data, during or after experimentation, is human-participant research needing IRB pre-approval, with adult consent. Teacher input during design, before experimentation, is not. The single-adult-tester exemption does not apply, because rating explanations does not require an adult tester.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/engineering-and-invention-projects-guide/>
- [medium] (Recording system performance in a classroom) Inference from the definitions:
- Exempt: the student researcher alone runs the system on photos showing only apparatus and on their own readings, with no human variable measured.
- Human-participant research needing IRB: classmates or a teacher operate or test the app; their lab readings or errors are analyzed; ratings or surveys are collected; class behavior is observed (a classroom is a restricted-access setting); or images capture identifiable people.
- Grey case: past records or photos supplied by a teacher are exempt only as certified de-identified pre-existing data.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/>
- [high] (Apps, AI responses and disclosing results to participants) The Companion Guide says:
- Student-designed apps tested or used by others are human-participant projects under all the HP rules.
- Results the app produces for participants must not be disclosed to them.
- Students are strongly advised not to use AI as a chatbot or to give unscripted responses to participants, even with guardrails.

The rules separately forbid disclosing study results or data to participants and giving advice or diagnostic/medical information. For 2026-27 the second clause was edited to bar this unless a medical professional supervises.  <https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/FINAL-Rule-Modifications-for-2026-27.pdf>
- [high] (Consent, assent and parental permission) A consent process is always required, and participation may start only after voluntary informed consent or assent. Rules for participants under 18:
- They always need written minor assent plus written parental permission.
- Any survey must be attached to the parental-permission form, and permission obtained before the minor sees it.

Rules for adults:
- Adults consent for themselves.
- The IRB may waive written documentation (not consent itself) only for minimal-risk, anonymous studies, e.g., normal educational practices.

Participants must be told they may stop at any time with no consequence, and there can be no coercion. A teacher requiring students to take part is a serious violation. Consent forms are signed after IRB approval and before that person participates.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/>
- [high] (Photos of students) Information that identifies participants, including photographs, may not be published or displayed without their written consent. At ISEF, photos or images of anyone other than the finalist need signed photo/video release forms. These are kept in the notebook, shown on request, and not displayed. The abstract form has a checkbox for unpublished photos of other humans on the board, and the Companion Guide says such images can prompt an SRC check for unapproved human-participant work. Completed consent or assent forms must never be in the exhibit hall.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/display-safety-rules/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Forms/2027-22-Categories.pdf>
- [high] (Privacy when participants are peers) When participants are the researcher's peers, extra privacy protection is expected. An adult can collect the data and pass it on de-identified. Small samples from an identifiable group, such as one class, threaten anonymity even in aggregate. Applicable privacy laws apply (FERPA and HIPAA are the US examples). Projects done outside the US must also follow local law.  <https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/>
- [high] (Forms required for every project and when) Every project needs:
- Form 1 (Adult Sponsor checklist), completed before experimentation;
- Form 1A (Student Checklist), completed before experimentation;
- the Research Plan/Project Addendum, written before experimentation;
- Form 1B (Approval), signed by the student and a parent before experimentation, with SRC final approval after experimentation and shortly before competition;
- Form 2A (Student Support Disclosure), new for 2027, which may be filled in before or after.

Form 1A records whether pre-approval is needed, a tentative start date, actual start and end dates, whether it is a continuation, and the data source. The Companion Guide says forms should be signed before any research; only Forms 2C, 5B and 7 and the abstract are done afterwards. The ISEF SRC answers rules questions year-round at SRC@societyforscience.org, and the online Rules Wizard lists the forms a project needs.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Forms/All.pdf; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf>
- [high] (Prior SRC approval scope and risk assessment) Prior IRB/SRC approval is required only for human participants, vertebrate animals, potentially hazardous biological agents and tissue. All other projects are reviewed by the SRC after experimentation and before the fair. Risk Assessment Form 3 is recommended for every project and must be done before experimentation. It is required whenever humans test a student-designed invention, prototype or product. Form 1 marks human testing of student-designed inventions as needing prior IRB approval.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/human-participants/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Forms/All.pdf>
- [high] (Research plan contents) The research plan must cover:
- rationale;
- research question(s), hypotheses, engineering goal(s) and expected outcomes;
- materials;
- procedures, separating the student's steps from any adult's;
- risk and safety;
- data analysis;
- bibliography;
- acknowledgements, where any AI support is documented with a prompt log or an explanation.

Human-participant plans add participants, recruitment, methods, risks, privacy and the consent process. Engineering plans may start less detailed, but iterative changes must be recorded in a Project Addendum.  <https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Forms/All.pdf; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/FINAL-Rule-Modifications-for-2026-27.pdf>
- [high] (Plan freeze after the affiliated fair) After competing at an affiliated fair, a project cannot be changed or amended, so the approved plan must include every phase. Between the fair and ISEF the student may only collect more data with the method already approved, and active research must stay within 12 months of an 18-month period. At ISEF 2026, projects failed to qualify for:
- human-participant work without IRB pre-approval;
- adding research that was not in the original plan;
- switching between individual and team projects midway.

The SRC also flagged as core problems plans that did not match the work actually done, unclear human testing, and overstated student work.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2026/Rules/SRC-Symposia-Slides.pdf>
- [high] (Research window and start date) A project may include at most 12 months of continuous research and no research done before January 2026. Judging covers only experimentation and data collection within 12 continuous months starting no earlier than January 2026 and ending by May 2027. The start date is when data collection begins; the literature review and study design come before it. Actual dates should match the lab notebook.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf>
- [medium] (Applying the window to a project started June 2026) A June 2026 data-collection start falls inside the window as long as the active period stays within 12 continuous months, i.e., ends by May 2027. However, Forms 1, 1A and 1B and the plan are supposed to be dated before experimentation. If they were not signed in June 2026, the student can either use that start date (and the forms' dates will not line up) or define a new ISEF phase that starts once the forms are signed and declare the June-Oct 2026 work as prior research. Which one the SRC accepts is for the SRC to decide.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Forms/All.pdf>
- [high] (Continuation projects (Form 7)) Any project built on the student's earlier research may be a continuation, and must show a substantive expansion such as a new variable or a new line of investigation. Re-running the same method on the same question, even with more samples, is not acceptable. Requirements:
- Form 7 can be completed after experimentation.
- It must come with the previous abstract and research plan, and it must be displayed at the ISEF booth.
- The board and abstract show only the current year's work; earlier work may appear only as summary conclusions compared with this year, with no prior raw data.
- The title may state which year of a study it is.

The Companion Guide says Form 7 is needed even if the earlier work was not in the immediately preceding year or was never entered in a competition. If in doubt, declare it. Saying no and then being found to have similar prior work is an ethics problem.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Forms/All.pdf; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf>
- [high] (Continuation enforcement) The ISEF 2026 SRC reported that many continuations were not declared. Where a study draws on prior research in the same field, it leans toward asking for continuation paperwork, and it sent about 40 such requests after reviewing students' prior project materials. The Rules FAQ says judges see declaring a continuation as normal, not as a negative.  <https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2026/Rules/SRC-Symposia-Slides.pdf; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/>
- [high] (Generative AI: the core rule) Rule 8 for all projects, new for 2026-27: AI may be used as a resource but must be cited and acknowledged, and everything presented must be in the student's own words. Generative AI must not be used to write the research plan, abstract or poster, or to create citations. The Ethics Statement lists inappropriate use of AI, alongside fraud and plagiarism, as grounds to fail to qualify.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/FINAL-Rule-Modifications-for-2026-27.pdf; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf>
- [high] (Generative AI use table) The Society/ISEF SRC AI use table (Oct 2025, reproduced in the 2027 Companion Guide).

Acceptable:
- Summarizing an article before reading it: no citation needed.
- Using a chatbot to develop ideas: keep a prompt log; citation may be needed.
- Writing initial code: only with explicit citation of which code is AI-generated plus a prompt log.
- AI-made figures: clearly marked and cited.
- Help choosing statistical tests or software tools: prompt log required; the student must interpret the data.
- Grammar and syntax polishing of a text the student wrote: no citation.
- Fixing bibliography formatting: references must be verified by the student.

Never acceptable:
- AI writing the first draft of the plan, abstract, paper or poster (refining a finished draft later is allowed with citation and a log).
- Adding points to the paper.
- Producing conclusions or future steps.
- Collecting data and writing the plan.
- Supplying citations or a starter bibliography.
- Summaries of sources the student never read.  <https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2026/Rules/Generative-AI-Use-Table.pdf; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf>
- [high] (AI disclosure on Form 2A) Form 2A, required for all projects from 2027, asks whether generative AI was used. If yes, the student attaches a list of the tools and which parts of the project used them, plus an AI prompt log with prompts and responses (personal information removed), or a summary if the log is unavailable. The form also lists every supporting adult and any paid support. The Companion Guide warns that omitting support received can be treated as misconduct.  <https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Forms/All.pdf; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf>
- [high] (AI graphics and citation integrity) Every graphic the finalist made must be credited with the software used and the year. Graphics from others need individual citations, with a URL or DOI. AI-created graphics must be labelled as AI-made, with the date and the tool; AI-modified graphics also cite the original. The Companion Guide discourages using AI to format references and requires manual checking of each one. In ISEF 2026 integrity checks, about 10% of projects had two or more citations that could not be verified.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/display-safety-rules/; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf; https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2026/Rules/SRC-Symposia-Slides.pdf>
- [high] (Booth and digital demo rules) Booth and demo rules:
- The exhibit must be viewable during all hall hours without relying on electricity or internet.
- An active internet connection used to display or run the project is banned unless the Display & Safety committee approves it in advance by email.
- Any screen-based material (slides, code, software or simulation) must be showable in full and may not change after the Display & Safety inspection.
- QR codes, URLs and email or social-media addresses are not allowed on the display.
- Commercial logos are allowed only if integral to the project and approved.
- Mentors and institutions may be named only in the acknowledgements.  <https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/display-safety-rules/; https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/>
- [high] (Abstract) After experimentation the student writes an abstract of at most 250 words on one page, describing only their own work in their own words (not written by AI). ## IMPLICATIONS
- 把ISEF的「真实技术升级」定位在方向(a)：为六个实验建立参数化的系统误差假设库，每个假设是带噪声模型的物理前向模型；用边际似然或BIC给假设排序，用EIG推荐下一次最有区分力的测量。论文要写明各部件的先例（GDE 1987、Box-Hill 1967、Blais 2020、Kok & Priemer 2023、BoxingGym 2025），新意落在「中学力学领域+真实课堂数据+闭环」，不要声称EIG或模型比较本身是新的。
- 似然中的噪声项直接用文献值，不要拍脑袋：人工起停计时σ约0.10 s（单端偏差0.11 s，Faux & Godolphin 2019）；刻度读数u=δ/√12，或用半宽a得u=a/√3（GUM/NPL）；米尺±1 mm（WJEC）。弹簧测力计分度值以学生照片里的实际器材为准。
- 故障假设要有可区分的数据特征，并可在干净的仿真数据上注入，得到带真值的评测集，从而绕开不能拍新照片的限制。例：牛二摩擦平衡不足或过度会使a-F截距变化，m不远小于M会使图线下弯（δ=(m+m_e)/M，何述平2016）；把斜长当高度是乘性因子1/sinθ；零点偏移是加性常数；漏掉转动动能会使ΔEk系统性偏小（Kok & Priemer 2023）；秒表反应时增大方差并可能带来偏差。
- 把固定的「相对误差<15%即一致」改为不确定度归一化的比较（t′或贝叶斯因子），并把「数据是否与理想模型一致」和「最可能是哪种系统误差」分开报告。这与Holmes & Bonn 2015和Holmes等2015 PNAS的PER主张一致，也直接对应高中课标「分析误差并提出减小误差的方法」的学业要求。
- 方向(b)升级仿真器用途：从只画理想过程动画，改为对每个故障假设生成反事实曲线（「如果是摩擦未平衡，你的数据会是这样」），叠加在学生真实数据上。这类似Tracker的模型叠加，但由系统自动生成。配合POE：先让学生预测误差来源，再看数据，最后看系统解释。
- 如果要在LLM侧做训练：在RTX 5090上用代码现场生成仿真或注入故障的合成数据，只上传代码，符合100 KB/s限制。以贝叶斯诊断器或物理计算器的输出一致性作为可验证奖励，做拒绝采样微调或RLVR，比较「未微调 vs 微调 vs API基线」的诊断讲解准确率。注意Sim2Reason（2026，MuJoCo+5%容差、Qwen系列）已走通仿真器RLVR，新意必须放在「从噪声学生数据诊断系统误差、讲解忠实于计算结果」这个任务上。
- 方向(c)：实验识别输出改为共形预测集合，而不是单一argmax（零样本VLM可参考Silva-Rodríguez CVPR 2025；LM Studio若不给logprobs，可参考Su等2024的采样法）。集合里有多个候选时，用EIG选一个最能区分它们的追问（CLAM思路），把cart+track三实验平分变成「有覆盖保证的集合+主动追问」。开集情况下允许输出「未知实验」，由VLM起草物理模型，再经人工确认。
- 不需新照片的反事实评测：利用809张图已有的YOLO框做器材级遮挡或移除，构造最小对（如去掉悬挂重物、去掉测力计，实验标签应改变或保持），同时评测规则层和本地VLM。若做「Hidden in plain sight」式的隐藏层探测，需要transformers或MLX取激活，LM Studio可能不提供，且必须与产品错开运行以守住24 GB上限。
- 不要让VLM读仪表示数：MeasureBench最好的模型在真实图像上只有30.3%，QuantiPhy显示VLM估计运动量多靠先验。继续手动输入读数，另加一道读数合理性检查（如超量程、与分度值不匹配）。
- 方向(d)的LLM讲解必须「计算先行、LLM只解释已核验的结论」。实验课GPT-4助手超过1/10的回答有害（Kregear等2025），约20%的LLM反馈有误且学生难以察觉（Maus等2025），PhysicsAssistant质量低于GPT-4。课堂实践中应统计讲解错误率，作为可报告的指标。
- 与PhysicsAssistant（YOLOv8+GPT，8年级实验课）明确区分：本项目现有的「YOLO+LLM」管线本身不新，ISEF叙事应突出物理模型驱动的系统误差诊断、不确定度量化和课堂闭环。
- 课堂评估（11/7起）可做小规模对照：有诊断与无诊断，或先预测与不预测（依据Crouch 2004）。结果变量为学生能否正确指出并解释系统误差，可改编Etkina科学能力量表或PLIC中的条目；诊断准确率以教师标注为金标准，前沿API只作基线。
- 内存预算：Qwen3.8-27B 4-bit约16-19 GB（Unsloth文档），在24 GB总上限下只能运行单实例，YOLO、Streamlit和贝叶斯计算要压在剩余约5 GB内。贝叶斯诊断与EIG用解析或网格计算即可，不需要额外模型。
## UNKNOWNS
- 人教版初中教材中「测量平均速度」（如斜面坡度宜小以便计时、从车头量到车头）和「探究滑动摩擦力」（如拉木板法）的原文误差说明，在线无法核实，需要对照纸质教材或教师用书。
- 学校弹簧测力计的实际量程和分度值（常见说法为0.1 N或0.2 N）没有找到权威标准，只有电商商品页；需从器材实物或已有照片确认。
- 弹簧测力计竖直调零后水平使用产生的零点偏移，没有找到定量文献值（只有零点误差属于系统误差的一般说法）。
- Faux & Godolphin的数据来自受过训练的游泳计时员，中学生的反应时偏差和方差可能更大；没有找到中学生秒表计时的直接文献值。
- 视差误差没有通用定量文献值。NPL只有定性说明；一份大学讲义称米尺读数可达±1-3 mm，未采信。
- 高中课标原文PDF未能读取，必做实验清单和误差要求来自教师网站转载；初中清单来自引用课标的解读文章。

- 24 GB上限下Qwen3.8-27B 4-bit加视觉编码器的实际峰值内存（图像token、KV缓存）未实测，文档值为16-19 GB。

## SUMMARY
Qwen3.8-27B exists. Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B (Apache-2.0) has a Hugging Face repo created 2026-08-05, and its weights went public around 2026-08-14. It is a dense, natively multimodal (image and video) checkpoint with 27.78B parameters. It uses the Qwen3.5 hybrid architecture: 48 Gated-DeltaNet layers plus 16 gated-attention layers, a 262K native context and an MTP head. Its architecture is identical to Qwen3.6-27B. Thinking is on by default. chat_template_kwargs enable_thinking=false turns it off, and reasoning_effort takes xhigh, medium or low.

The student's 16.08 GB file is exactly lmstudio-community/Qwen3.8-27B-MLX-4bit, LM Studio key qwen/qwen3.8-27b. It uses affine 4-bit quantization with group size 64, keeps the vision tower in bf16 and drops the MTP head. "Bionic" is LM Studio's separate agent app, launched 2026-07-16; it is not a publisher or quantizer. This Mac has Bionic.app 1.1.0+11, no LM Studio.app, and no LLM downloaded yet.

The LM Studio runtime offers OpenAI-compatible and native REST APIs with image input and JSON-schema output. The lms CLI covers get, load (with -c, --ttl, --estimate-only) and unload, plus JIT loading, TTL and auto-evict. LM Studio cannot load LoRA adapters.

Community runs of the same architecture on an M4 Pro (20-core GPU, 48 GB) measured about 112-123 tok/s prefill, 13.7-15.6 tok/s decode, and 15.8-17.3 GB peak memory at 1-4k context. My estimate for an 8k context plus one 1024-px image (1,024 visual tokens) is about 18.5-21 GB total and 13-15 s to first token. A raw 12 MP photo would become about 12k tokens.

mlx-lm and mlx-vlm both support LoRA/QLoRA for qwen3_5. mlx-vlm keeps the vision tower; mlx-lm drops it. A 27B QLoRA under 24 GB on the Mac is unproven. On an RTX 5090, Unsloth says QLoRA of Qwen3.8-27B fits in 24 GB, but its Qwen3.5 guide advises against QLoRA for this family. A rank-16 adapter is about 160-235 MB in bf16. Smaller siblings are the official Qwen3.5-0.8B, 2B, 4B and 9B, all with vision.

Bionic 1.1.4 mentions Chat Completions and Responses APIs.  <https://lmstudio.ai/changelog>
- [high] (LM Studio OpenAI-compatible and native endpoints) OpenAI-compatible endpoints: GET /v1/models, POST /v1/responses, /v1/chat/completions, /v1/embeddings, /v1/completions; the docs assume base URL http://localhost:1234/v1. The native REST API v1 has POST /api/v1/chat, /api/v1/models/load (accepts context_length), /api/v1/models/unload and /api/v1/models/download, with optional API-token auth.  <https://lmstudio.ai/docs/developer/openai-compat; https://lmstudio.ai/docs/developer/rest; https://lmstudio.ai/docs/developer/rest/load>
- [high] (LM Studio image input) Native /api/v1/chat accepts input items {"type":"image","data_url":"data:image/png;base64,..."}. The docs describe the OpenAI-compatible Chat Completions as handling 'text and images'. The server supports JPEG, PNG and WebP images.  <https://lmstudio.ai/docs/developer/rest/chat; https://lmstudio.ai/llms.txt; https://lmstudio.ai/docs/python/llm-prediction/image-input>
- [high] (LM Studio structured output) /v1/chat/completions accepts response_format {"type":"json_schema","json_schema":{...}} and returns the JSON as a string in choices[0].message.content. GGUF models use llama.cpp grammar sampling and MLX models use Outlines. The docs warn that models under 7B may not handle structured output well. In LM Studio's open-source mlx-engine (main branch), vision models use mlx-vlm's JSON-schema logits processor.  <https://lmstudio.ai/docs/developer/openai-compat/structured-output; https://github.com/lmstudio-ai/mlx-engine/blob/main/mlx_engine/generate.py>
- [medium] (Disabling thinking in LM Studio) Native /api/v1/chat has a 'reasoning' parameter ("off"|"low"|"medium"|"high"|"on") and a per-request context_length. Open bug lmstudio-bug-tracker #1990 (opened 2026-05-31, LM Studio 0.4.15+2) reports enableThinking=false ignored via the OpenAI-compatible API for Qwen3.5-9B GGUF. A 2026-10-16 comment says "reasoning": "off" on /api/v1/chat worked for Qwen3.5 9B. Behavior for Qwen3.8 in the installed runtime is untested.  <https://lmstudio.ai/docs/developer/rest/chat; https://github.com/lmstudio-ai/lmstudio-bug-tracker/issues/1990>
- [high] (lms CLI commands) Download: `lms get <model>`, with '@quant' for a specific quantization, --mlx/--gguf filters, -a to choose a quantization, or a full Hugging Face URL. Load: `lms load <key> -c/--context-length N --gpu max --ttl SECONDS --parallel N --identifier ID -y`. `--estimate-only` prints a memory estimate without loading. The installed CLI also has speculative flags (--speculative-draft-mtp, --speculative-draft-simple --speculative-draft-model). Unload: `lms unload [key] [--all]`. Also `lms ps`, `lms server start [-p port] [--bind 127.0.0.1]`, `lms import <file>` and `lms runtime select`.  <https://lmstudio.ai/docs/cli; https://lmstudio.ai/docs/cli/local-models/load; https://lmstudio.ai/docs/cli/local-models/get>
- [high] (JIT loading, TTL, auto-evict) JIT loading is on by default. JIT-loaded models get a default idle TTL of 60 minutes, and a per-request 'ttl' field overrides it. Models loaded with `lms load` have no TTL unless --ttl is given. Auto-Evict is on by default and keeps at most one JIT-loaded model in memory; it does not affect models loaded by other means.  <https://lmstudio.ai/docs/developer/core/ttl-and-auto-evict>
- [medium] (LM Studio and LoRA adapters) LM Studio does not load LoRA adapters. lmstudio-ai/mlx-engine issue #51 'LoRA support' has been open since 2024-11-28, with user requests continuing through 2026-08-28. The workaround is a fused model placed in ~/.lmstudio/models/<publisher>/<model>/ or imported with `lms import`.  <https://github.com/lmstudio-ai/mlx-engine/issues/51; https://lmstudio.ai/docs/app/advanced/import-model; https://lmstudio.ai/docs/cli/local-models/import>
- [medium] (LM Studio MLX engine limits for VLMs) In mlx-engine (main branch), vision models load through an mlx-vlm batched path that 'does not support KV cache quantization yet'. A code comment says max_image_size is 'legacy-only; batched VLM lets mlx-vlm processors resize', meaning images are resized only by the model's preprocessor limits.  <https://github.com/lmstudio-ai/mlx-engine/blob/main/mlx_engine/generate.py>
- [high] (Image token counts) The preprocessor uses patch 16 and merge 2, so one visual token covers 32x32 px. min_pixels is 65,536 and max_pixels is 16,777,216. So a 1024x1024 image is about 1,024 tokens and 1024x768 about 768. A 4032x3024 (12 MP) phone photo stays under the cap and becomes about 11,970 tokens, which would overflow an 8k context.  <https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B/raw/main/preprocessor_config.json; https://huggingface.co/lmstudio-community/Qwen3.8-27B-MLX-4bit/raw/main/processor_config.json>
- [medium] (KV cache and recurrent state size) Computed from config: KV cache = 16 full-attention layers x 2 x 4 KV heads x 256 x 2 bytes = 64 KiB per token, so 8,192 tokens is about 0.54 GB (262k tokens would be about 16 GiB). The fixed Gated-DeltaNet state is 48 layers x 48x128x128 fp32, about 151 MB, independent of context length.  <https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B/raw/main/config.json>
- [medium] (Measured speed and memory on M4 Pro (same architecture)) oMLX community benchmarks of Qwen3.6-27B 4-bit on an M4 Pro (20-core GPU, 48 GB). At 1k context: prefill 111.7-123.1 tok/s, decode 13.7-15.6 tok/s, TTFT 8.3-9.2 s, peak 15.8 GB. At 4k: prefill 83.1-122.1 tok/s, decode 12.8-15.1 tok/s, peak 16.8-17.3 GB. At 64k: prefill 98.9 tok/s, decode 11.6 tok/s, peak 26.4 GB. Batch-4 aggregate decode was 23.6-34.0 tok/s.  <https://omlx.ai/benchmarks/2vypcx8z; https://omlx.ai/benchmarks/kx5b6fdb; https://omlx.ai/benchmarks/5kxvj7fd>
- [medium] (Bandwidth-bound decode estimate) M4 Pro memory bandwidth is 273 GB/s. About 14.4 GB of weights are read per decoded token (16.05 GB minus the 0.93 GB vision tower and the ~0.7 GB embedding table, only one row of which is read). That gives a ceiling of about 19 tok/s, so the measured 13.7-15.6 tok/s is roughly 72-82% of the bound.  <https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/10/apple-introduces-m4-pro-and-m4-max/; https://huggingface.co/lmstudio-community/Qwen3.8-27B-MLX-4bit>
- [low] (Total memory estimate: 8k context plus one 1024 px image) Estimate about 18.5-21 GB: weights 16.05 GB (incl. bf16 vision), KV 0.54 GB, DeltaNet state 0.15 GB, prefill and vision-encoder transients about 1-2.5 GB (measured peaks grow 1.0-1.5 GB from 1k to 4k), and runtime overhead about 0.5-1 GB (the idle Bionic service alone is 0.51 GB). That leaves only about 3-5.5 GB under a 24 GB cap for Streamlit, YOLOv8s and a browser.  <https://omlx.ai/benchmarks/2vypcx8z; https://omlx.ai/benchmarks/kx5b6fdb; https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B/raw/main/config.json>
- [low] (Image prefill time estimate) For 4,096 patches, the vision encoder (27 layers, hidden 1152) needs about 5.5 TFLOP, roughly 1-1.5 s at ~4-5.5 effective TFLOPS (third-party FP32 peak for the 20-core M4 Pro GPU is about 8.1 TFLOPS). The LLM prefill of the 1,024 visual tokens at the measured ~115 tok/s takes about 9 s. Time to first token for one 1024-px image plus ~400 text tokens is therefore about 13-15 s.  <https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B/raw/main/config.json; https://omlx.ai/benchmarks/kx5b6fdb; https://nanoreview.net/en/gpu-compare/apple-m6-gpu-12-core-vs-apple-m4-pro-gpu-20-core>
- [medium] (Speculative decoding options) mlx-vlm supports DFlash2 speculative decoding for Qwen3.8-27B (drafter z-lab/Qwen3.8-27B-DFlash2: 1.92B params, 3.85 GB bf16) and claims '~2x or higher throughput'. mlx-vlm notes 'Structured outputs are not currently supported with speculative decoding.'  <https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/blob/main/README.md; https://huggingface.co/z-lab/Qwen3.8-27B-DFlash2>
- [medium] (Splash engine (Inco AI)) Splash is an open-source (Apache-2.0) local inference engine for Apple silicon. It supports Qwen3.8-27B via incoai/Qwen3.8-27B-Splash (a 17.4 GB package with 4-bit target, DFlash2 draft and bf16 vision encoder) or from mlx-community/Qwen3.8-27B-4bit / unsloth GGUF. It offers OpenAI-compatible APIs with JSON Schema output and base64 images; reasoning_effort 'none' disables thinking. It requires an M3 or newer chip and macOS 26.4+; the 27B package needs 36 GB of unified memory (48 GB recommended), and a --max-memory cap is available. Vendor-measured 27B decode is 74 tok/s on an M5 Pro and 92 tok/s on an M3 Max, versus 16-17 tok/s for llama.cpp (coding prompts, greedy). There are no M4 Pro numbers.  <https://huggingface.co/incoai/Qwen3.8-27B-Splash; https://github.com/incoai/splash; https://github.com/incoai/splash/blob/main/docs/performance.md>
- [high] (mlx-lm LoRA/QLoRA support) mlx-lm (0.32.2, 2026-10-16) has a qwen3_5 model implementation. Its LoRA converts every Linear/QuantizedLinear layer in the last num_layers blocks; if the base is quantized, training is QLoRA. Defaults: rank 8, scale 20, num_layers 16, batch_size 4, max_seq_length 2048, grad_checkpoint off. The LoRA config uses 'scale', not 'alpha'.  <https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/models/qwen3_5.py; https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/tuner/utils.py; https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/lora.py>
- [medium] (mlx-lm fuse pitfalls for this VLM) mlx-lm's qwen3_5 loader skips vision_tower/model.visual weights and drops mtp.* weights, so `mlx_lm.fuse` on this model would produce a text-only model. --export-gguf supports only llama/mixtral/mistral. LoRALinear.fuse dequantizes the base weight, adds the LoRA delta and re-quantizes with the same bits and group size, so fusing into a 4-bit MLX model is possible without the bf16 base.  <https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/models/qwen3_5.py; https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/fuse.py; https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/tuner/lora.py>
- [medium] (Gated DeltaNet training path on MLX) In mlx-lm, training calls gated_delta_update with use_kernel=not self.training, which runs a per-token Python loop over [B,48,128,128] fp32 states (about 3.1 MB per step per layer). That is slow and memory-heavy for long sequences. mlx-vlm's training path instead uses a chunked parallel scan (chunk size 64).  <https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/models/gated_delta.py; https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/models/qwen3_5.py; https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/blob/main/mlx_vlm/models/qwen3_5/gated_delta.py>
- [high] (mlx-vlm training and serving) mlx-vlm 0.7.11 (PyPI, 2026-10-28) provides `python -m mlx_vlm.train` with SFT/ORPO/DPO/GRPO, lora/dora/full training, --train-vision, --quantization-bits 4/8, --grad-checkpoint (shown in its GRPO example), and default lora-rank 8 / alpha 16. It also supports Qwen3.5 native MTP-head training. mlx_vlm.server can serve a base model plus adapter with --adapter-path. The server also offers OpenAI-compatible json_schema outputs (including with images), logprobs (top_logprobs up to 20), --max-pixels, --kv-bits, and thinking off by default.  <https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/blob/main/docs/training.md; https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/blob/main/mlx_vlm/train.py; https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/blob/main/README.md>
- [low] (QLoRA memory on Mac (27B vs smaller)) No published MLX QLoRA memory figure for Qwen3.5/3.6/3.8-27B was found. My estimate for batch 1, ≤1k tokens with gradient checkpointing is about 19-22 GB: 16 GB base, 1-2 GB LoRA parameters plus AdamW state, and 2-4 GB activations and 248k-vocab logits. That is borderline for a 24 GB cap. Qwen3.5-9B (about 6 GB at 4-bit) and 4B (about 3 GB) would fit comfortably.  <https://huggingface.co/lmstudio-community/Qwen3.5-9B-MLX-4bit; https://huggingface.co/lmstudio-community/Qwen3.5-4B-MLX-4bit; https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/LORA.md>
- [medium] (Unsloth on RTX 5090) Unsloth's Qwen3.8 guide says 'QLoRA works with 24GB and LoRA needs >36GB'. It uses unsloth/Qwen3.8-27B-unsloth-bnb-4bit (22.36 GB download). Its example uses max_seq_length 2048, r=16, adamw_8bit and offload_embedding. Vision fine-tuning is enabled with finetune_vision_layers=True. Export options are GGUF, merged 16-bit, or LoRA-only. It advises keeping at least 75% reasoning data to preserve reasoning, and notes the first run can be slow while Gated DeltaNet kernels compile. So QLoRA of the 27B fits in 32 GB; bf16 LoRA does not.  <https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.8/train; https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.8-27B-unsloth-bnb-4bit>
- [high] (Unsloth QLoRA caveat and bf16 LoRA VRAM) Unsloth's Qwen3.5 guide lists bf16 LoRA VRAM as 0.8B 3 GB, 2B 5 GB, 4B 10 GB, 9B 22 GB, 27B 56 GB. It also says 'It is not recommended to do QLoRA (4-bit) training on the Qwen3.5 models, no matter MoE or dense, due to higher than normal quantization differences'. That conflicts with the Qwen3.8 guide's QLoRA recommendation, since both models use the same architecture.  <https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/fine-tune>
- [high] (RTX 5090 specs) The GeForce RTX 5090 has 32 GB of GDDR7 on a 512-bit bus with 1,792 GB/s bandwidth.  <https://www.nvidia.com/en-in/geforce/graphics-cards/50-series/rtx-5090/; https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/graphics-cards/compare/>
- [high] (ms-swift support) ms-swift (4.6.0, 2026-10-13) lists Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B and -FP8 with model_type qwen3_5, default template qwen3_8, Megatron support and tags vision, video. It requires transformers>=5.2.0 (the best-practice page installs transformers>=5.9, flash-linear-attention>=0.4.2 and causal-conv1d). The Qwen3.8 template supports reasoning_effort via chat_template_kwargs. Example LoRA uses rank 8, alpha 32, all-linear targets. No 27B-on-32 GB memory figure is published.  <https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/docs/source_en/Instruction/Supported-models-and-datasets.md; https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/docs/source_en/BestPractices/Qwen3_8-Best-Practice.md; https://pypi.org/project/ms-swift/>
- [medium] (Adapter file sizes (computed)) From config dimensions: Qwen3.8-27B LoRA over all language-model linears has about 7.30M parameters per unit of rank. r=8 gives about 117 MB in bf16; r=16 about 233 MB bf16 (467 MB fp32); r=32 about 467 MB bf16. If only q/k/v/o and MLP layers are targeted, r=16 is about 159 MB. For comparison at r=16: Qwen3.5-9B about 87 MB and Qwen3.5-4B about 65 MB (bf16). At 100 KB/s, 233 MB takes about 39 minutes.  <https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B/raw/main/config.json; https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B/raw/main/config.json; https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B/raw/main/config.json>
- [medium] (PEFT to MLX adapter conversion) mlx-vlm LoRALinear stores lora_a as (in, r) and lora_b as (r, out) and computes y + scale * x @ a @ b, with scale defaulting to 20. PEFT/Unsloth adapters store lora_A (r, in) and lora_B (out, r) with scaling alpha/r. Using a server-trained adapter on the Mac therefore needs key renaming, transposes and scale mapping. No official converter was found. mlx-vlm LoRALinear also has a fuse() that dequantizes, adds and re-quantizes.  <https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/blob/main/mlx_vlm/trainer/peft/lora_layers.py; https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/tuner/utils.py>
- [low] (MLX on CUDA) MLX has a CUDA backend (pip install mlx[cuda12] or mlx[cuda13]; Nvidia architecture SM 7.5 or newer; CUDA 13 needs driver 580+). Community reports describe problems on consumer Blackwell (sm_120), e.g. fp32 matmul about 36x slower than cuBLAS on an RTX 5070 Laptop. That makes PyTorch-based Unsloth or ms-swift the safer training stack on the 5090.  <https://ml-explore.github.io/mlx/build/html/install.html; https://upd.dev/ml-explore/mlx/issues/3861>
- [high] (Smaller official siblings) Qwen3.5-0.8B, 2B, 4B and 9B are official, Apache-2.0, qwen3_5 architecture, natively multimodal (vision and video), with 262,144-token context. The 4B and 9B think by default; the 2B runs non-thinking by default. The Qwen3.5 card advises keeping at least 128K context to preserve thinking quality.  <https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B; https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B; https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B>
- [high] (Smaller siblings: sizes and LM Studio availability) lmstudio-community MLX 4-bit sizes: 9B 5.98 GB, 4B 3.06 GB, 2B 1.75 GB. GGUF Q4_K_M plus mmproj: 9B 5.63+0.92 GB, 4B 2.71+0.68 GB, 2B 1.27+0.67 GB. The LM Studio catalog lists qwen/qwen3.5-2b (2.00 GB), -4b (3.75 GB), -9b (7.00 GB), -27b and -35b-a3b; all support vision input, tool use and reasoning, in GGUF and MLX. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (MoE, about 3B active) is 20.43 GB at MLX 4-bit, too big for 8-16 GB laptops.  <https://huggingface.co/lmstudio-community/Qwen3.5-9B-MLX-4bit; https://huggingface.co/lmstudio-community/Qwen3.5-4B-MLX-4bit; https://huggingface.co/lmstudio-community/Qwen3.5-2B-MLX-4bit>

NVFP4 builds exist, e.g. nvidia/Qwen3.8-27B-NVFP4 (21.95 GB, tagged text-generation) and unsloth/Qwen3.8-27B-NVFP4 (23.44 GB). ## IMPLICATIONS
- Use lmstudio-community/Qwen3.8-27B-MLX-4bit (the 16.08 GB file). After downloading, run `lms load --estimate-only <key> -c 8192` first, then `lms load <key> -c 8192 --parallel 1 --ttl 900 --gpu max`. Budget about 19-21 GB for the LLM, which leaves only about 3-5 GB under the 24 GB cap for Streamlit, YOLOv8s and the browser. Measure real peak memory before the 11-09 classroom use.
- Downscale every photo before sending it to the VLM (longest side about 1024 px gives ≤1,024 visual tokens; 768 px gives about 430-576), or send YOLO crops. A raw 12 MP phone photo becomes about 12k tokens: it overflows an 8k context and would need about 100 s of prefill. Accuracy versus image resolution and latency is itself a clean, measurable ISEF experiment.
- Run classroom calls in non-thinking mode (temperature 0.7, top_p 0.8, top_k 20, presence_penalty 1.5). At about 14 tok/s, a thinking trace adds minutes per answer, and a JSON-schema constraint would collide with the open <think> block. Use LM Studio's /api/v1/chat with reasoning:"off" or set Enable Thinking=false as the model default. Add an automated check that the reply has no reasoning content, because thinking-off through the OpenAI-compatible path has open bug reports.
- Latency budget per photo on the 27B: about 13-15 s to first token, then about 14 tok/s. Keep the VLM output a compact JSON: experiment-type enum including 'other/unknown', apparatus list and confidence. Stream short explanations (about 150-250 tokens). Consider Qwen3.5-9B (6 GB) for the interactive loop and the 27B for verification. The two cannot stay loaded together under 24 GB, so rely on JIT plus auto-evict/TTL.
- Open-set recognition: JSON-schema output with an enum of the 6 curriculum experiments plus a free-text 'other' field always parses, which keeps manual selection compatible. If calibrated confidence or abstention is needed, mlx-vlm's server exposes token logprobs; LM Studio's documented API does not list logprobs.
- A credible 'real technical upgrade' within the constraints: (a) LoRA/QLoRA on the rented RTX 5090 using data generated on the server by code. Unsloth says 27B QLoRA fits in 24 GB; bf16 LoRA on Qwen3.5-9B needs about 22 GB and avoids the QLoRA caveat. (b) GRPO with verifiable physics rewards computed by physics.py-style code; Unsloth, ms-swift and mlx-vlm all support GRPO. Evaluate before and after on held-out classroom photos and readings. Frontier APIs serve only as baselines, not as training-data generators.
- Adapter logistics: keep rank ≤16 (27B r=16 is about 160-235 MB in bf16; 9B about 60-90 MB) so adapters can be downloaded over a slow link. Never plan to transfer merged models (16-17 GB) or the bf16 base (55.6 GB, more than the 38 GiB of free disk).
- Deploying a fine-tuned model: LM Studio cannot load adapters. Option 1: serve base plus adapter with `mlx_vlm.server --adapter-path` (keeps vision, JSON schema and logprobs; thinking is off by default). Option 2: fuse into the 4-bit MLX model with mlx-vlm's LoRA fuse (dequantize, add, re-quantize) and place the result in ~/.lmstudio/models/<publisher>/<name>/. Do not use mlx_lm.fuse, which drops the vision tower. Budget time to write a small PEFT-to-MLX converter (rename keys, transpose, map alpha/r to scale). Re-validate quality, because the adapter is trained against bnb-4bit weights, not MLX affine 4-bit.
- On-Mac training: prefer mlx-vlm's trainer (chunked DeltaNet scan, --grad-checkpoint, --quantization-bits) over mlx-lm, whose qwen3_5 training path loops per token. Prototype on Qwen3.5-4B/9B. Treat 27B QLoRA under 24 GB as unproven: run an early memory probe at sequence length ≤1024, batch 1.
- School laptops: offer Qwen3.5-4B (3.1 GB MLX; 3.4 GB GGUF plus mmproj) for 8 GB machines and Qwen3.5-9B (6.0 GB / 6.6 GB) for 16 GB machines. Both have vision, but LM Studio warns that sub-7B models are weaker at structured output, so keep the existing rule-based path as a fallback.
- Housekeeping before 11-06: update Bionic (installed 1.1.0+11; changelog is at 1.1.9) or install LM Studio 0.4.26 or newer for the developer server. Select the newer MLX runtime (1.13.1 is installed but 1.11.0 is selected). Download the model early (16 GB) and keep ≥20 GB free disk for one fused copy.
- Optional speed path: Splash or mlx-vlm DFlash2 speculative decoding claims about 2x decode for Qwen3.8-27B, but neither has been measured on an M4 Pro. Splash's 27B package expects a machine with ≥36 GB unified memory and must be capped with --max-memory. mlx-vlm turns off JSON-schema output when speculating. Use these only as a measured experiment, not as the default.
- If bandwidth is the only reason data cannot be uploaded: re-encoding the 809 images to about 512 px JPEG (roughly 30-60 KB each, 25-50 MB total) would take about 5-8 minutes at 100 KB/s. This is an inference; check whether the constraint has other reasons, such as privacy.

## SUMMARY
Wikimedia Commons has a few thousand freely licensed files of physics apparatus, but few of them show modern school-lab setups. Direct (non-recursive) API counts: Physics experiments 300 files with 55 subcategories, Physics education 66, School laboratories 71, Spring scales 72, Dynamometers 130, Hooke's law 55, Inclined planes 40, Pendulums 114, Pulleys 370, Optical benches 33, Refraction 256, Van de Graaff generators 111, Electroscopes 68, Calorimeters 89, Breadboards 85. Recursive (deepcat) and keyword counts are badly inflated because the category tree and keywords drift into unrelated subjects: Friction leads to Brakes, Inclined planes to playground slides, and the German 'schiefe Ebene' to a railway incline. Every sampled file was CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or public domain. Many files are diagrams, museum pieces or industrial machines. Chinese apparatus terms (弹簧测力计 spring scale, 打点计时器 ticker timer) return zero results. The most useful Commons assets are about 110 videos labelled by topic in their titles: about 50 Getaclass physics lessons (CC BY 3.0) and 63 Macedonian physics experiment videos (CC BY-SA 4.0). Both come with 480p transcodes suitable for frame extraction. Thumbnails can be fetched via imageinfo with iiurlwidth (rounded to a standard size such as 1280) plus extmetadata for attribution. Limits: 200 API requests/min; for media, at most 2 parallel downloads and 25 Mbps. Open image datasets: two CC BY 4.0 instrument sets from United International University, Dhaka (PhysiSens, 5,185 images/20 classes; Figshare, 5,121 images/27 classes, about 190-200 MB each) download anonymously. The Roboflow sets found are mostly electrical circuits and need an API key. Video/VLM benchmarks: Physics-IQ (396 real videos, CC BY), PhysBench (10,002 items mixing web, real and simulated media; 7.4 GB), PhysLab (real university labs, only partly released, no license), and the synthetic Physion, CLEVRER and IntPhys 2 (IntPhys 2 is non-commercial only, CC BY-NC). No open dataset of raw school-student measurements for the six experiments was found. Literature can calibrate the noise model: manual timing error N(0.11 s, 0.07²), σ_g = 0.28 m/s² across 271 student measurements of g, and a 5.7% photogate bias. numpy simulation with deliberately injected errors is trivial on the Mac. MuJoCo has Apple Silicon wheels and renders off-screen (CGL); PyBullet has no Apple Silicon wheel on PyPI; photoreal Blender/Kubric rendering costs too much before 11-09.

## IMPLICATIONS
- Build two evaluation tiers. Tier 1 (closed set): the existing 809 images plus the five classroom sessions. Tier 2 (open set): about 300-800 hand-curated Commons stills plus keyframes from about 110 topic-labelled Getaclass and Wikiexperiments videos. Treat video titles only as candidate labels and verify every one by hand. Report open-set accuracy and an 'unknown experiment' rejection metric such as AUROC, with the six curriculum experiments as known classes.
- Harvest Commons on the Mac with small API calls: metadata JSON plus 960/1280 px thumburls (about 0.2-0.4 MB each, so 1-2k images stay under about 1 GB). Use a contact User-Agent, at most 2 parallel media downloads and 25 Mbps, and dedupe by sha1. Do not walk category trees recursively without per-subcategory review, because the trees drift into unrelated subjects.
- Write a data card or manifest (title, author, source URL, license, license URL) for every Commons and Openverse item. Prefer CC0/PD/CC BY/CC BY-SA items. Keep NC/ND Openverse items out entirely, or use them only for evaluation and never redistribute or modify them.
- Do not expect Commons to improve in-domain detection of the six classes. It has essentially no Chinese-curriculum kit (spring scales, ticker timers, carts). Use it for generalization, robustness and open-set tests, not as YOLO training data for the six classes.
- PhysiSens and the Figshare 27-class set (CC BY 4.0, about 0.4 GB together, no account) can be fetched directly on the GPU server. They are a cheap test of open-vocabulary detection (instrument names) and distractor/negative data. Helical-Extension-Spring, Weight-carrier, Brass-Scale-Weights and Retort-Stand overlap with a Hooke's-law setup.
- Fetch the large benchmarks only on the GPU server, never on the Mac (39 GB free disk, 16 GB taken by the LLM): PhysBench 7.4 GB, ExpVid 41.7 GB, SciVideoBench 22.4 GB, CLEVRER 12.4 GB. Physics-IQ (real, CC BY) and PhysBench (mixed) can be small-subset baselines comparing local vs frontier VLMs on physical understanding. Respect the Physics-IQ rule against tuning on its test data.
- For the error-diagnosis engine, use synthetic fault injection with ground-truth error labels per experiment. Examples: uncompensated friction or m not much less than M (Newton's 2nd law); spring-scale zero offset, an off-axis pull or reading while accelerating (friction); exceeding the elastic limit or ignoring the initial length (Hooke's law); timing reaction error drawn from N(0.11, 0.07²) per action (average speed); an unlevelled track or friction loss (momentum); drag on the tape (energy). Calibrate the noise against the literature, then report per-error-type precision and recall.
- Validate realism externally: check that the simulated spread of a timing-based estimate matches published student distributions (σ_g about 0.28 m/s² over 271 student values), and include a calibration-bias case such as the 5.7% photogate bias. Then validate on real typed readings from the five classroom sessions, if they were logged. That gives a closed loop of simulate, diagnose and check against real classes.
- Keep synthetic generation to numpy (negligible RAM) on the Mac, alongside the roughly 16 GB 4-bit LLM, inside the 24 GB cap. MuJoCo (25 MB wheel, CGL off-screen rendering) is optional for animations or idealized-vs-measured visual comparisons. Skip PyBullet on the Mac, and treat Blender/Kubric photoreal rendering as out of scope before 11-09 (asset modelling takes days, and Kubric needs Linux/Docker, which is only possible on the GPU after 11-06).
- Circuit datasets with explicit wrong-wiring classes (Roboflow SMT 'rheostat(wrong)') suggest how a later electricity extension could frame visual error diagnosis. They need a Roboflow API key, which the student would create and keep on the server as an environment variable.
